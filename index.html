What to Expect

Every Berklee undergraduate applicant completes an interview (≈15 minutes). For Bachelor of Music and BA in Black Music & Culture it is a live conversation (in-person or Zoom). For BA in Music Industry Leadership & Innovation it is recorded via Kira Talent.

Official focus areas (from Berklee Admissions):
  1. What motivates you to pursue a career in music? Who are you as an artist? What excites you about the industry?
  2. Community & belonging: What does community mean to you? Why is a safe/welcoming environment important? What can Berklee learn from you?
  3. Why Berklee? Which programs/activities align with your career goals?

All interviews are conducted in English. Language skills are assessed if English is not your first language. Authenticity, clear goals, and specific knowledge of Berklee matter more than perfect grammar.

Duration: ≈15 minutes after your audition (or standalone for BA Industry). Be ready 30 minutes early for online sessions.

Typical Interview Questions + Best Ways to Answer

1. What motivates you to pursue a career in music / performing arts?
Strong answer structure:
“I am motivated by the power of music to connect people and tell stories. Growing up in [your city/country], I discovered that composing/performing allowed me to express emotions I couldn’t put into words. What drives me is the desire to create music that moves audiences and to keep growing as an artist every day.”

Tip: Be personal and specific. Mention a real moment or influence.
2. Who are you as an artist?
“As an artist I am a [your primary identity – e.g., songwriter who blends Latin rhythms with jazz harmony / guitarist focused on improvisation and original composition]. My style combines [2–3 concrete elements]. I value authenticity, collaboration, and continuous learning.”
3. What excites you about the music industry?
“I am excited by how technology and global collaboration are changing the way music is created and shared. The opportunity to work with artists from different cultures and to reach audiences worldwide motivates me to keep improving my craft and understanding of the business side.”
4. What does community mean to you?
“Community means a group of people who support each other’s growth, share knowledge, and create a safe space where everyone can be themselves. In music, community is essential because we grow faster when we play, write, and learn together.”
5. Why is a safe and welcoming environment important to you?
“Creativity needs safety. When people feel respected and included, they take artistic risks and share honest ideas. A welcoming environment allows every voice to contribute, which makes the music and the learning richer for everyone.”
6. What can the Berklee community learn from you?
“I can share my experience with [your cultural background / unique musical tradition / perspective on collaboration or mental health in music]. I bring a strong work ethic, openness to feedback, and a desire to help other students grow through collaboration and mutual support.”
7. Why Berklee? What about Berklee’s programs speak to your career goals?
“Berklee is the place where I can combine high-level performance training with real-world music business and technology skills. I am especially interested in [specific major or courses, e.g., Contemporary Writing & Production / Music Production & Engineering / Professional Music]. The opportunity to study with faculty who are active professionals and to collaborate with students from all over the world matches exactly the career path I want: becoming a versatile artist and creator who can work across styles and platforms.”

Pro tip: Mention 1–2 specific programs, ensembles, or facilities. Show you researched the school.
Extra common questions:
• How have you prepared for Berklee?
• What are your professional / career goals after graduation?
• Tell me about a challenge you overcame in your musical journey.
• Which musicians or styles influence you most?

Final Tips to Excel

  • Be authentic – Berklee values real stories over perfect answers.
  • Research specific programs – Name 1–2 majors, courses, or ensembles that match your goals.
  • Practice out loud – The more you speak your answers, the more natural they sound.
  • Show community mindset – Talk about collaboration, support, and what you will contribute.
  • Eye contact & energy – Smile, thank the interviewer, and stay present.
  • For online interviews – Test camera, microphone, lighting, and internet in advance. Use a quiet room.
  • Prepare 2–3 concrete examples from your musical journey (a performance, a challenge, a collaboration).
  • End strong – You can ask a thoughtful question about student life or a specific program if time allows.
Remember: The interview is a conversation. They already know you can play (from the audition). Now they want to know who you are and why Berklee is the right next step for you.
